Executive summary

Same brief, new origin. You asked whether the Squam program — a ~$2M property, a party lodge at ~$250K, five sleeping cabins at ~$100K each, on clear forested water where towsports are legal and normal — works within four to eight hours of Chicago. It does, and on the whole it works better here than in New England, for one structural reason: the Northwoods still has hundreds of grandfathered mom-and-pop resorts, and their owners are retiring. In the Northeast the compound has to be assembled. In Wisconsin and Michigan it is frequently already standing, already zoned for multiple dwellings, and on the market for less than a single house on Squam.

The headline: Donner's Bay Resort on the Turtle–Flambeau Flowage — six updated year-round cabins, an owner's lodge and a licensed bar and grill, asking $1.57M on 12,942 acres of state-designated Scenic Waters with 195 islands and a shoreline that can never be developed. That is the sketch in your concept doc, in budget, today. Its one honest flaw is the water: the Turtle–Flambeau is tea-stained rather than gin-clear. If clarity is non-negotiable, the answer changes to Grindstone Lake near Hayward — 3,176 acres with thirteen years of 23-foot Secchi readings, the truest Squam analog west of Lake Michigan. Be warned that clear water and a standing cabin cluster rarely appear on the same lake here: on Grindstone and on Round Lake next door, the buildings exist but almost nothing is listed at any moment.

On the Michigan side, the value is different in kind. Elk Lake (7,730 acres, 192 feet deep) is Torch Lake's water without Torch Lake's sandbar. Crystal Lake in Benzie County is the best water-to-drive ratio in this entire report — 9,854 turquoise acres, 165 feet deep, five and a quarter hours from the Loop. And Mullett and Douglas Lakes in Cheboygan County offer high-quality deep water at roughly half the per-foot price of the Torch–Walloon corridor an hour west.

Three moves to make this fall:

  • Book one Northwoods resort weekend: tour Donner's Bay on the Turtle–Flambeau, Pine Valley Lodge outside Rhinelander (27 acres, 1,900 feet of frontage, $1.5M) and Moose Jaw at Park Falls — three complete programs inside a two-hour triangle, on three different grades of water. Sleep on Grindstone and see the difference for yourselves.
  • Book one northern-Michigan loop anchored on Elk Lake and Crystal Lake, with a calibration day on Torch (to see what the money buys) and a value day on Mullett and Douglas.
  • Start the quiet-market machinery now: the resort brokers — JMDS, Traverse North, Cabins & Camps, Eliason — see retiring-owner listings before MLS does, and the good ones trade in weeks. Get on their lists in September, not April.

What we're solving for

Trout Lake, Wisconsin — the second archetype in your original brief — is 3,816 acres, 117 feet deep, alkaline and unusually transparent, and sits almost wholly inside the 236,000-acre Northern Highland–American Legion State Forest. Like Squam, it is quiet for structural reasons rather than regulatory ones: the state owns the shoreline, the launch is modest, and the private frontage that exists has been in the same families for generations. Skiing is legal; the boats are simply outnumbered. That combination is what this search is for.

Hard filters used:

  • Four to eight hours from the Loop. The floor matters as much as the ceiling: everything inside four hours — Lake Geneva, Delavan, Elkhart, Big Green, the Chain O'Lakes — is Chicago's own weekend water, priced and trafficked accordingly. Green Lake in particular is a genuinely excellent lake (237 feet deep, the deepest in Wisconsin) excluded purely on the drive floor and its growing crowds.
  • Waterskiing and cruising legal and practical — no electric-only lakes, no horsepower caps. Neither state restricts towsports on any lake in this report, though both are actively debating wake-boat setbacks (see the compound math below).
  • Documented clarity, forested and substantially conserved shoreline, low traffic. The marquee-busy lakes (Minocqua, the Eagle River chain, Torch's sandbar, Higgins' south shore, the main basin of Lake Charlevoix) were excluded or heavily caveated on principle.
  • ~450+ acres, so five boats aren't tripping over each other — with explicit exceptions where the water is exceptional (Black Oak, Thumb Lake, Chicaugon, Lake Owen).
  • Room for the program: property ~$2M or less (stretch ~$2.75M turnkey), able to host a main lodge plus five sleeping cabins — which makes former resorts, cabin colonies and large-frontage parcels disproportionately valuable.
  • May–October use; seasonal buildings are fine. Note that Northwoods "three-season" often means genuinely unheated: budget for it.

The compound math (read before you shop)

The single most important fact in this report is a zoning fact, and it is not obvious from a listing site. Wisconsin shoreland zoning does not let you build your sketch on one lot. Under NR 115 and the county ordinances that implement it, an unsewered lake lot needs at least 100 feet of average width and 20,000 square feet, buildings sit back 75 feet from the ordinary high-water mark, the first 35 feet inland is a no-cut vegetative buffer, and impervious surface is capped around 15%. On top of that, Vilas County — and most of its neighbors — permits one principal dwelling plus one non-rental guest house per parcel. A lodge and five cabins is six dwelling units. On a single residential lot it is simply not permittable.

There are exactly four ways through, and they define the whole strategy:

  • Buy a grandfathered resort. Licensed resorts carry commercial or recreational zoning and pre-existing cabin density that no one could permit today. This is the cheapest, fastest and most certain route, and it is the reason this report leans so heavily on resort listings. Confirm in writing with the county that the density survives a change to private family use — some ordinances tie it to continued commercial operation.
  • Assemble contiguous lots. Three adjacent 200-foot lots is three principal dwellings plus three guest houses — six units, legally, with 600 feet of frontage. This is why the big-frontage land plays in this report (Lac du Lune at 1,181 ft, Pine Valley Lodge at 1,900 ft, the Phelps 1,022-ft assemblage) matter more than their acreage suggests.
  • Condominium-plat it. A residential condominium can carry multiple dwelling units on common land where fee-simple subdivision could not. Standard practice in the Northwoods; needs a Wisconsin land-use attorney from day one.
  • Go to Michigan. Michigan has no statewide shoreland zoning for inland lakes at all — density is set township by township, and Part 301 of NREPA only regulates work below the water and within 500 feet of it. Some townships are permissive; others have adopted keyhole/funnel ordinances specifically to stop multi-family lake access. You must ask, in writing, before you offer.

What things cost. Northwoods renovation math is friendlier than New England's: your $100K per cabin genuinely renovates a 400–700 sq ft resort cottage here, and often buys a new insulated shell on piers. Land basis varies more than most buyers expect — Cheboygan County land runs roughly $12K–$17K per acre against Antrim County's $18K–$28K, and Lake Charlevoix frontage runs about $2,450 per linear foot in the South Arm against $7,000–$12,000 on the main basin. In Wisconsin the county averages tell the same story: Vilas lake listings averaged about $750K this summer, Minocqua $951K, Eagle River $659K, and Bayfield County just $627K.

Tier 1 — pursue these six

1. Grindstone Lake, Sawyer County, Wisconsin — the truest Squam analog west of Lake Michigan

3,176 acres, 60 feet deep, 10.5 miles of shoreline, and — the number that matters — a Secchi transparency that averaged 23 feet across thirteen consecutive years of monitoring. That is Kezar-grade water on a lake big enough for five boats, six hours and twenty minutes from the Loop. The shoreline is a mix of understated old family camps and Lac Courte Oreilles Band land; there is one landing; the culture is generational rather than rental. Live now: a four-season chalet paired with a three-season guest cottage on a level sand-beach lot — two buildings already standing, which under Wisconsin's one-dwelling-plus-one-guest-house rule is the maximum a single parcel gives you. Strategy: buy that, then buy the neighbour. Title work on this lake needs a Hayward attorney who has handled reservation-adjacent parcels; that is not a reason to avoid it, but it is a reason not to use your Chicago firm.

2. Turtle–Flambeau Flowage, Iron County, Wisconsin — where the program is already built

12,942 acres, 195 islands, and a state Scenic Waters designation that has kept essentially the entire shoreline undeveloped and undevelopable (WI DNR). It is the wildest big water within seven hours of Chicago, and it is the only lake in either of these reports where your exact sketch is currently for sale at a price that leaves change: Donner's Bay Resort, six updated year-round cabins plus an owner's home plus a 1,500 sq ft bar and grill, at $1.57M. Six cabins, a lodge, a commercial kitchen and a liquor licence — for less than one ordinary cottage on Crystal Lake. Be honest about the tradeoff: this is a flowage, so the water is tannin-brown rather than clear, there are stumps, and levels are managed. If your group weights wildness and program over clarity, stop reading and book the tour. If clarity is the point, go to Grindstone.

3. Elk Lake, Antrim County, Michigan — Torch Lake's water, without Torch Lake's weekend

7,730 acres, 192 feet deep, the same improbable Caribbean blue as its famous neighbour, and a shoreline of old cottages rather than a nationally-known party anchorage. Elk connects through the Chain of Lakes to Torch and Bellaire, so you can visit the scene and then leave it. Five hours twenty-five from Chicago — this is the northern-Michigan answer for a group that wants beauty without the Torch premium or the Torch crowd. The compound math is the constraint: Antrim County land runs $18K–$28K per acre and lots are small. The play is a rare acreage parcel — a 14+ acre, 4BR in the Torch–Elk corridor is live at $999K, and a 7.5-acre estate parcel between the two lakes is on the market — plus township confirmation on multiple dwellings before you offer.

4. Round Lake, Sawyer County, Wisconsin — the buildable Trout Lake, when it trades

3,294 acres and 74 feet deep, wrapped by the Chequamegon National Forest northeast of Hayward: state-forest atmosphere with private frontage that does, occasionally, come to market. The one two-building listing — 12391 W Moreland Road, a furnished 4BR cabin plus a guest cottage on sugar-sand frontage at $1.95M, which is the legal maximum for a single Wisconsin parcel — went pending on September 1. Nothing else is live. So Round is a relationships lake, not a bidding lake: get known by the Hayward brokers, ask to be called if Moreland Road falls through, and send owner letters. When it does trade, this is the clearest big ski water in the state with buildings already on it.

Correction, September 1: an earlier draft of this briefing placed Moose Jaw Resort on this lake. It is not — Moose Jaw sits on the other Round Lake, in the Pike Chain near Park Falls in Price County, on 726 acres of 24-foot root-beer-coloured water. It is still a strong program buy; it is not a clear-water buy. See the honest program-versus-water note below.

5. Crystal Lake, Benzie County, Michigan — the best water-to-drive ratio in the report

9,854 acres, 165 feet deep, 21 miles of shoreline, and turquoise water over a white marl bottom that has earned it the local name "the Caribbean of the North" (Crystal Lake Watershed Association). Five hours and fifteen minutes from the Loop — closer than every Wisconsin lake on this list and clearer than almost all of them. The catch is the shape of the market rather than the price level: Beulah frontage is small-lot and ordinary cottages ask $1.2M–$1.9M, so a compound here means assembling two or three adjacent parcels or siting cabins on a back lot. Do the assembly and you will own the most beautiful, most accessible water in this report; skip it and you will visit often anyway.

6. Lac Courte Oreilles, Sawyer County, Wisconsin — the biggest clear lake you can still afford

5,039 acres, 90 feet deep, 25.4 miles of shoreline, and water the Wisconsin DNR classifies as very clear — the largest genuinely clear ski lake in this report that still trades at compound prices. Twenty-five miles of shoreline buys real variety: quiet coves, sand beaches, and enough open water that five boats never see each other. A legacy estate with 10.82 acres and 320 feet of level sand frontage is live at $2.495M, which is enough land to site the lodge on the water and cluster cabins uphill of the 75-foot setback on separately-platted lots. It is twenty minutes from Grindstone, so a single Hayward campaign covers both. Two honest caveats: parts of the shore sit within or adjacent to the Lac Courte Oreilles Band's reservation and the title work is genuinely specialist, and the lake is big enough that it is lively rather than hushed on an August Saturday.

Tier 2 — strong candidates, one tradeoff each

Lake Tomahawk, WI (~5:30). The best combination of size (3,392 ac), depth (84 ft), clarity and drive time in Wisconsin, an hour closer than the Vilas cluster. A legacy estate with a main home, furnished cabins and multiple docks is live at $3.3M. Tradeoff: stretch pricing, and busier than Trout.

Presque Isle Lake, WI (~6:05). 1,165 acres and 103 feet deep in the far northwest corner of Vilas County, half an hour past where day-trippers turn around. Thirty active listings town-wide averaging $520K makes it the most likely place in Wisconsin for the multi-lot assemblage strategy to actually work. Tradeoff: under the size bar, and genuinely remote.

Mullett & Douglas Lakes, MI (~6:15). The Cheboygan County value pair. Mullett is 16,630 acres and 145 feet deep — Michigan's fifth-largest, remarkably underdeveloped for its size, and on the Inland Waterway to Lake Huron. Douglas is 3,727 acres wrapped by the University of Michigan Biological Station, with no village, no commercial shoreline, and clarity the Tip of the Mitt Watershed Council describes as bordering on oligotrophy. Land here runs $12K–$17K per acre against Antrim's $18K–$28K. Tradeoff: an hour further than Traverse City and a plainer landscape.

Higgins Lake, MI (~5:25). The clearest big water within five and a half hours of Chicago — Secchi readings of 28.5 to 35 feet across the basins, 9,600 acres, 135 feet deep (HLPOA). It is also the busiest lake on this shortlist: two state parks, a fully developed ring road, and midsummer Saturdays that feel metropolitan. Buy on the north or east shore or don't buy here. Frontage runs roughly $1M–$1.9M.

Big Glen Lake, MI (~5:30). 4,871 acres and 130 feet deep inside Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore — federal land on much of the shore and the most photographed water in the Midwest. Very few homes trade in a given year and $2M–$3M buys one house, not a compound. Tour it; buy elsewhere unless the group's budget moves.

Lake Owen, WI (~6:45). 1,250 acres, 95 feet deep, 24.6-foot Secchi, national forest on most of the shore. Archetype water on a sub-archetype footprint, with a top-heavy market — a 5.3-acre, 350-foot retreat asks $3M. Watch for the middle of the market; it exists, rarely.

Chippewa Flowage, WI (~6:20). 15,300 acres, 140 islands, 92 feet deep and more than 200 miles of shoreline that can never be developed. Seventeen live listings. The largest wild water in this report, with the same stained-water caveat as the Turtle–Flambeau. If the group's fantasy is scale and solitude rather than a gin-clear swim, this and the Turtle–Flambeau are the two serious candidates.

Lake Metonga & Lake Lucerne, WI (~5:15). Forest County is the sweep's quiet discovery: Metonga is 2,157 acres and 80 feet deep at Crandon, Lucerne is 1,000 clear acres in the Nicolet National Forest fifteen minutes away, and neither is on any Chicago buyer's list. Cheapest clear water inside five and a half hours. Tradeoff: almost no inventory at any moment — this is a saved-search-and-wait lake.

Chicaugon Lake, MI (~6:15). 1,100 acres and 115 feet deep outside Iron River, with an improving clarity trend and an active property owners association. Western-UP pricing is the argument: a seven-acre waterfront parcel on nearby Hagerman Lake is $425K and a 3.5-acre, 367-foot Iron County parcel is $129.9K. Assemble three of those and your land basis for the whole compound is under $400K. Tradeoff: under the size bar, and a long way from a grocery store.

Les Cheneaux Islands, MI (~7:00). The outlier, and for some groups the answer: 36 islands and a dozen sheltered channels on northern Lake Huron, a century-old wooden-boat culture, no crowds, and prices a third of Torch Lake's. A private island with 575 feet of frontage, a 3BR historic cottage and a three-stall boathouse is live at $1.0M; a Cedarville cottage-plus-annex-plus-boathouse plus a back lot with a barn is $799K. Tradeoffs are real: Great Lakes weather, a shorter season, seven hours of driving, and water that is cold in June.

Watchlist, edge cases, and honest exclusions

Trout Lake itself: we checked, and the answer is the same one Squam gave. There were zero lake properties for sale on Trout Lake this summer. The state forest owns nearly all of it; what private frontage exists does not come to market. Watch it, set an alert with the Boulder Junction brokers, and plan on something else. Its nearest buyable substitutes are Round Lake (national forest ring, real inventory), Presque Isle Lake, and Boulder Lake — 542 clear acres inside the same state forest, right next door.

Torch Lake is the report's sharpest split decision. On paper it is the best water in the Midwest — 18,770 acres, 330 feet deep, ranked among the clearest lakes in the Western Hemisphere. In practice, the south sandbar is a nationally known party anchorage with hundreds of rafted boats on a July Saturday. The lake passes; the sandbar's culture does not. Buy on the quiet northeast shore or treat Elk Lake as the substitute. Walloon Lake is the Midwest's Squam in both senses: oligotrophic, no commercial shoreline, generational owners — and twelve listings totalling $64.7M this summer, with entry cottages in the mid-$800Ks and real frontage at $7M+. Tour it to calibrate, then go to Douglas or Mullett.

Worth monitoring: Thumb Lake / Lake Louise, MI (470 acres of ~90-foot spring-fed water, among the clearest in Michigan, far under the size bar); Black Oak Lake, WI (Wisconsin's clearest, one 3BR live at $699K, essentially no other inventory in any year); Green Lake at Interlochen, MI (1,980 acres, 100 feet deep, five hours flat, consistently overlooked); the Manitowish Waters chain, WI (ten connected lakes that trade far more often than any archetype lake in Vilas County); and Grand Lake, MI (5,660 spring-fed acres on the Presque Isle peninsula that the Chicago market has simply never noticed).

The honest program-versus-water problem. Four of the best complete compounds found in this sweep sit on lakes that fail the water or towsports bar outright, and they are listed on the map with explicit caveats rather than as recommendations: Halfway Lake Cottages near Newberry (eight cabins, a guest house and an owner's residence on 25 acres and 1,700 feet of frontage for $1.595M — on a private no-motor lake); the Herendeene Lake resort between Sleeping Bear and Traverse City (a hand-hewn log home, a six-bedroom farmhouse and six remodelled cabins for $2.5M — on a 40-acre no-wake pond); two separate complete cabin clusters on Houghton Lake at $1.19M and $1.5M (20,044 acres, but only 21 feet deep and heavily trafficked); and Moose Jaw Resort at Park Falls (eight cabins, 18 campsites, 24 boat slips and a licensed bar on 17 national-forest acres for $2.75M — on 726 acres of 24-foot, root-beer-coloured Pike Chain water with moderately high phosphorus). Tour at least one of them. They are the fastest way to learn what your program actually feels like when it is finished — and what you would be giving up to have it now.

Why nothing closer to Chicago made the list. Inside four hours the water that exists is either developed to the waterline (Lake Geneva, Delavan, Elkhart, the Chain O'Lakes, Gun and Gull Lakes in Michigan), shallow and wind-driven (Winnebago, Houghton, Otsego), or a drawdown flowage (Castle Rock, Petenwell, Lake Wisconsin). Big Green Lake is the one genuine loss — 237 feet deep, Wisconsin's deepest, and excluded only by the four-hour floor and its rising traffic. Past eight hours the picture improves again and then keeps improving: Lake Vermilion, Burntside and the Gunflint lakes in Minnesota are the truest Trout Lake analogs in North America, and they sit at eight and a half to nine and a half hours. If the group ever softens the ceiling by ninety minutes, that is where to start.

  • Northern Michigan loop: Crystal Lake → Big Glen → Elk Lake → Torch (calibration only) → Walloon (calibration only) → Douglas and Mullett (the value day). Brokers: Traverse North, Brook Walsh, Jones North.
  • The wildcard weekend: Les Cheneaux Islands, with a Drummond Island day if the ferry doesn't put anyone off. Go in August, not June — the water is cold.

Working the resort market — the core strategy

  • Register with the specialist desks, not the general portals. JMDS Properties (WI/MN resorts), Traverse North Realty and Cabins & Camps (Michigan campgrounds and cabin resorts), and Eliason Realty (Cisco Chain and Vilas). These desks see retiring-owner listings first, and the good ones trade in weeks.
  • Understand the demographic trade you are making. Northern Wisconsin's cottage-resort stock has been shrinking for sixty years: between 1961 and 1968 alone, Oneida County lost 23.4% of its resort-type establishments against a 13.9% statewide decline, and roughly 98% of the losses were seasonal cottage-type places (Monthey & Zielinski, 1973). The pattern has not reversed — the usual buyer today demolishes the cabins to build one house. Five couples who buy a resort and keep the cabins are the exception, and that is precisely the arbitrage: you are paying for buildings the market discounts.
  • The timing is unusually good. Wisconsin's vacation-home share of mortgage originations fell from 4.9% in 2021 to 2.6% in 2025 — the second-home market has roughly halved while Northwoods tourism itself set records (Vilas and Oneida each cleared $400M of visitor impact in 2025). Sellers are patient, buyers are scarce, and the owners are still aging out.
  • Before you offer on any resort, get the county or township zoning administrator's written answer to one question: does the existing dwelling density survive a conversion from licensed resort to private family use? Everything depends on it. In Vilas County that is the Zoning & Planning Department — zoning@vilascountywi.gov, (715) 479-3620 — whose General Zoning Ordinance was last updated 15 December 2025. Ask for the current ordinance, not a cached copy.
  • Watch institutional dispositions. Scout councils, church camps and YMCA camps still sell periodically across the Northwoods and northern Michigan, and they carry the densest grandfathered cabin clusters of all.
  • Register for the Michigan DNR surplus land auction. The state sold 97 parcels this summer — over 20 of them 40 acres or larger, concentrated in exactly the counties on this list. It is the cheapest route to acreage in the report.
  • Set saved searches on the twenty shortlist lakes across LakePlace, LandSearch, LandFlip, LakeHomes and the county MLS feeds, and check weekly. Vilas alone carried 284 active lake listings this summer; the ones that matter last days.

Before any offer

  • Septic for twelve-plus bedrooms is the make-or-break item everywhere. Get a soil evaluator engaged during the inspection period, not after. On a converted resort, confirm whether the existing system was sized for commercial occupancy — sometimes it is oversized in your favour.
  • Get the dwelling-density answer in writing (see above). In Wisconsin also confirm the guest-house rule's non-rental condition against your intended use — if the group ever wants to rent, that changes the structure entirely.
  • Check the town or township wake-boat ordinance for your specific lake, not just state law. Eighty-plus Wisconsin municipalities have passed one, thirty-seven in 2025 alone, and the trend is one-directional.
  • Read the lake association's water-quality reports for your specific bay, and ask about invasives: Eurasian watermilfoil and starry stonewort are both moving through this region, and a management plan is a cost as well as a comfort.
  • In Wisconsin, remember the public-trust doctrine: you buy shoreline, never lake. There is no such thing as a private lake here, and any lake with a public landing has public traffic by right.
