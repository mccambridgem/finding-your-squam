Executive summary

You asked for a lake like Squam or Trout — clear, forested, quiet, loon-country water — over a lake that is merely close. The good news: that lake exists in at least a dozen places within about six and a half hours of Manhattan, and on several of them your program (a ~$2M property, a party lodge at ~$250K, five cabins at ~$100K each) is genuinely executable. The catch is that what makes these lakes feel like Squam — conserved shorelines, no big public boat ramp, generational owners — also makes their real-estate markets tiny. The winning strategy is less “browse Zillow” and more “pick two or three lakes, get known by their brokers and lake associations, and move fast when a camp surfaces.”

The headline: Seymour Lake in Vermont's Northeast Kingdom is your best odds of actually pulling this off — archetype-grade water (oligotrophic, invasive-free, 1,777 acres), a real waterski culture with a state-designated wakesports zone, and camp prices where a compound pencils out. Kezar Lake in Maine is the truest Squam analog on the map and worth a patient, relationship-driven pursuit. In New York, Raquette and Big Moose give you Great Camp DNA at attainable prices — including a live $1.2M listing on Big Moose. And if you want to buy land now and build the exact sketch in your concept doc, the live 43.9-acre, 596-foot-frontage parcel on Webb Lake, Maine ($985K) is the cleanest blank canvas we found.

Three moves to make this fall:

  • Book one scouting weekend in the Northeast Kingdom (Seymour, Echo, Willoughby, Caspian, Maidstone are all within ~35 minutes of each other) and tour the $1.2M four-dwelling compound near Willoughby while you're there.
  • Book one Adirondack loop (Piseco → Raquette → Big Moose → Upper Saranac) anchored by the Big Moose listing and a calibration tour of a full compound on Upper Saranac.
  • Start the quiet-market machinery on Kezar and Caspian: introduce the group to the two or three brokers who control those lakes, and send owner letters — the best camps there trade before they ever hit MLS (a Caspian camp sold in one day in late 2024).

What we're solving for

Decoding Squam and Trout Lake tells you exactly what to shop for. Both are big-but-not-huge (6,800 / 3,900 acres), genuinely clear (oligotrophic, 20+ feet of visibility), and quiet for structural reasons: large stretches of shoreline are conserved (the Squam Lakes Conservation Society; Wisconsin's Northern Highland state forest), public boat access is modest, and the culture is old camps handed down rather than rental turnover. Crucially, neither is a no-motor lake — skiing is legal and normal — the boats are just outnumbered by the loons. That combination, not proximity or amenities, is the archetype.

Hard filters used in this search:

  • Within ~6.5 hours' drive of Midtown Manhattan (your stated flex beyond the doc's 5 hours).
  • Waterskiing and motor cruising legal and practical — no electric-only lakes, no horsepower caps or speed limits that kill towsports. (Vermont's 2024 rule only constrains ballasted wakesurfing, and every Vermont lake on our shortlist has a designated wakesports zone; ordinary skiing and tubing are exempt statewide.)
  • Clear water by state monitoring data, forested and substantially conserved shorelines, low traffic — the busiest marquee lakes (Winnipesaukee, George, Sebago, Candlewood) were excluded on principle.
  • Room for the program: property ~$2M or less (stretch ~$2.5M turnkey), able to host a main lodge plus five sleeping cabins — which makes former camps, existing cottage clusters, and big parcels disproportionately valuable.
  • May–October use; seasonal buildings are fine, which keeps renovation budgets realistic.

The compound math (read before you shop)

Your budgets — $250K for the party space and $100K per cabin — are renovation numbers, not new-construction numbers. Custom lakefront building in northern New England runs roughly $350–600+ per square foot, so $100K builds about 200 square feet new but comfortably renovates a 400–600 sq ft camp cabin or buys a simple seasonal shell on piers that you finish yourselves. This is why the whole search tilts toward former summer camps, sporting camps, and properties with existing outbuildings: the buildings you need may already be standing, often with grandfathered rights you could not permit today. Your sketch — cabins clustered behind a shoreline lodge — is, conveniently, exactly what shoreland zoning rewards.

Tier 1 — pursue these four

1. Kezar Lake, Lovell, Maine — the truest Squam analog

Nine miles long, 2,665 acres, 155 ft deep, oligotrophic and getting clearer for fifty years (Kezar Lake Watershed Association), with the White Mountains stacked behind the western shore, Greater Lovell Land Trust holdings, an active loon program, no horsepower limits, and only small launch ramps — so the water stays hushed in a way even Squam sometimes doesn't. The culture is understated old camps, not show. The problem is supply: there are typically zero to two true waterfront listings at any moment; when they appear they run roughly $900K–$2.5M+ (Kezar Realty, CB Lifestyles). Maine's 200-ft-per-dwelling frontage rule means the compound wants 600+ ft of shore or cabins clustered uphill beyond the 250-ft zone. Strategy: meet the Lovell brokers this fall, join the KLWA mailing list, and send owner letters. When the right camp surfaces, this is the lake your grandchildren will refuse to sell.

2. Seymour Lake, Morgan, Vermont — best odds of executing the plan

The Northeast Kingdom's overlooked big lake: 1,777 acres, deep and cold, one of the Clyde system's two oligotrophic lakes, no invasives on record (Seymour Lake Association), a genuine ski-and-tube camp culture, and a state-designated 1,130-acre wakesports zone — the wake-boat couple is fully legal here (VT DEC wakesports rule). Camps list from the $200Ks; the benchmark comp is a main house + guest cottage + boathouse on 255 ft of frontage that sold for $1.25M, and a 27-acre two-home compound nearby went for $730K in June 2026. That is compound math with room to spare. The tradeoffs are honest: ~6:15 from Midtown and a region with more dirt roads than restaurants. Pair the trip with Echo Lake next door (the clearest water in the VT dataset, with a live $398K/100-ft home as a possible beachhead) — buy on one, ski both.

3. Raquette Lake, New York — Great Camp DNA with land to build on

The birthplace of the Adirondack Great Camp (Pine Knot, Sagamore), ~5,000 convoluted acres of bays, islands and mostly wild or state shoreline around a tiny hamlet, ~5:20 from Midtown. The water is clean with the faint Adirondack tea tint — a half-shade off Squam's gin, traded for the most storied camp culture in America. It's the rare archetype lake with attainable entries: this month's sweep found a $649K two-cabin property (a ready-made compound seed) and a $1.2M chalet with 200 ft of frontage (Raquette Lake listings). APA rules are the design constraint — frontage per principal building, but with the guest-cottage exemption, rustic-cabin carve-outs, and grandfathered pre-1973 camps as your levers. A patient buyer here can assemble exactly your sketch.

4. Big Moose Lake, New York — the hushed classic (with a live listing)

1,286 acres of dark-timber old camps and hotel-era lore northwest of Old Forge — clear water, almost no public-ramp traffic, and a culture so quiet it inspired both An American Tragedy and generations of one-family camps (Timm Associates). Live right now: 215 Judson Road — $1.2M, 10.4 acres, 200 ft of frontage, a 2006 post-and-beam 4BR plus a bunkhouse (verified active, MLS 205548). The asterisk: it's seasonal and boat-access-only, with HOA dock and parking on the mainland — magical for atmosphere, real friction for construction logistics and five-couple arrivals. If the group finds boat-in romantic rather than annoying, this is the strongest single listing on an archetype lake inside the budget.

Tier 2 — strong candidates, one tradeoff each

Merrymeeting Lake, NH (~4:45). Spring-fed and among NH's clearest, ~1,150 acres, no big public ramp so it's famously quiet; skiing legal and practiced. The closest true archetype water to the city. Tradeoff: modest inventory (homes $600K–$1.6M, plus a 12.5-acre land play at $312K) and a smaller playing field for five boats. Merrymeeting listings.

Great East Lake, NH/ME (~4:50). Secchi readings around 10 meters — some of the clearest water in either state (GELIA monitoring) — with low-key camp culture on the Wakefield/Acton line. A 45-year family camp is live at $994K. Tradeoffs: rising cyanobacteria counts as a watch item; Maine-side parcels carry the 200-ft rule. Lakes Region Realty — Great East.

Caspian Lake, VT (~5:45). Greensboro is the NEK's Squam-in-miniature — 790 crystal acres, Willey's Store, a summer culture a century deep. Nothing is listed; a 143-ft-frontage fishing camp sold in one day for $925K in late 2024. This is a letters-and-relationships lake — highest ceiling, least control. Jim Campbell Real Estate — Caspian.

Maidstone Lake, VT (~6:15). The most Trout-Lake-WI place in New England: 756 oligotrophic acres (Secchi 8.2 m) wrapped in state forest, nesting loons, one dirt access road. Grandfathered camps rarely trade and go fast; nothing on-lake is listed today. Watch it patiently alongside Seymour. Maidstone State Forest.

Upper Saranac Lake, NY (~5:15). The New York Squam — Great Camp architecture, Forest Preserve stretches, a nationally regarded milfoil dive program. But ≤$2.5M buys one camp, not a compound: the archetype 4-dwelling Panther Cove compound asks $3.7M. Tour it anyway to calibrate what “done” looks like. Panther Cove listing.

Upper Chateaugay Lake, NY (~5:50). The value arbitrage: deep, cold, clear far-north water with zero tourist economy. Startling prices — $599K for 4.3 acres and an 868-ft private cove; $699–995K for 4BR camps. Two adjacent purchases equals a compound for well under $2M. Tradeoff: sparse services, windswept, and the drive. LaPoint Realty — Chateaugay.

Piseco Lake, NY (~4:00). Best drive-to-wildness ratio anywhere: ~2,900 acres ringed by Forest Preserve and three DEC campgrounds, low-key ski culture, clear-ish water. Inventory is thin ($399K lakefront land typical; occasional $700K–$1.4M camps) but the four-hour drive makes casual scouting easy. Piseco Lake area.

Webb Lake, Weld, ME (~6:20). Good-not-elite clarity (shallower, ~40 ft max), but the setting is the region's wildest — Mount Blue State Park and Tumbledown conservation lands ring the valley. The live 43.9-acre, 596-ft-frontage parcel at $985K is the cleanest build-your-sketch canvas in this report: lodge near shore, five cabins beyond the 250-ft line, all-in near $2.2M with everything new. Watchlist, edge cases, and honest exclusions

Squam itself: we checked. Eleven listings ran $325K–$6.6M this month, but the sub-$500K entries are roadside or shared-access; usable Big Squam frontage starts around $3M and compound-scale parcels run $5M+. At ~$2M it isn't realistic — at double the budget, revisit Little Squam. Newfound Lake, NH has world-class clarity (27.7-ft readings in 2025, per NLRA) and big ski water at ~4:40, but the Route 3A shore is developed, summer traffic is moderate-high, waterfront starts ~$1.3M — and note the newly detected spiny water flea. A fine fallback if the group weights drive time harder.

Worth monitoring: Conway Lake, NH (1,298 quiet acres, conserved eastern shore, ~5:20, consistently underpriced); Thompson and Pleasant Lakes, ME (archetype water, near-zero current inventory); Embden Pond, ME (the cheapest clear water we found — camps in the $400Ks, a 53-acre parcel at $290K — at ~6:25); Lake Bonaparte and Chazy Lake, NY (generational-camp value); Echo Lake, VT (clearest Secchi in the VT dataset, $398K entry); Great Averill Pond, VT (an 830-acre oligotrophic secret with almost no market); and Lake Willoughby, VT — spectacular, but buildable private frontage barely exists (see the opportunities table for the one live wildcard).

Over the drive line, for the record: Mooselookmeguntic (16,320 wild acres wrapped by the Stephen Phillips preserve — the truest Trout Lake analog east of Wisconsin) and Rangeley are ~6:45–7:15 from Midtown. Current value there is remarkable — a 53.9-acre waterfront retreat with house and new barn at $725K; a 20.5-acre island camp at $459K. If the group ever softens the drive rule by 45 minutes, start here. Why nothing closer to NYC made the list: within three hours, the water that exists (Candlewood, Wallenpaupack, Greenwood) is trafficked and ringed with development; the Catskill lakes are mostly no-motor reservoirs; Otsego has recurring harmful algal blooms and estate pricing; Great Sacandaga is a drawdown reservoir with a speedboat scene. The archetype simply doesn't occur near the city — which you already sensed when you said distance was your flex.

The playbook

Scouting trips (each is one long weekend)

  • Northeast Kingdom loop: Seymour → Echo → Willoughby (tour LaCross Lane) → Caspian → Maidstone — five candidate lakes inside ~35 minutes of each other. Brokers: Jim Campbell RE, Century 21 Farm & Forest, Big Bear RE.
  • Adirondack loop: Piseco → Raquette (see the $649K two-cabin) → Big Moose (215 Judson Rd) → Upper Saranac (calibrate on Panther Cove) → optional Chateaugay extension. Brokers: Merrill L. Thomas, Timm Associates, Adirondack Premier Properties, LaPoint.
  • NH–Maine loop (pair with your next Squam visit): Merrymeeting → Great East ($994K camp) → Conway Lake → Kezar (meet Kezar Realty and Pinkham) → optional Webb/Embden day if the build-from-scratch path appeals.

Working the quiet market

  • Owner letters on Kezar, Caspian, and Maidstone: short, personal, five-families-one-lake story; source addresses from town tax rolls; time them for fall, when owners decide whether to carry another season.
  • Call the camp specialists: LandVest (ME/NH/VT/ADK camps desk), Dana Moos (Maine cottage-colony and sporting-camp broker), Morton & Furbish (Rangeley, if the radius grows). Ask specifically about retiring cottage-colony and kids'-camp owners — those deals surface off-MLS.
  • Watch institutional dispositions: Scout councils, church and Y camps still sell periodically in the ADK and Maine — grandfathered cabin clusters at land value. Adirondack Explorer has tracked the BSA sell-off.
  • Set saved searches (LakeHomes, LandWatch, Zillow, local MLS feeds) for the twelve shortlist lakes and check weekly; NEK and ADK camps have been moving in days.

Before any offer

  • Septic feasibility for ~12 bedrooms is the make-or-break diligence item everywhere; get a site evaluator engaged during attorney review.
  • Confirm the compound program with the town (and APA in the Adirondacks) in writing — grandfathered structures, guest-cottage rules, and rebuild rights vary lake to lake.
  • Check the lake association's water-quality reports (milfoil, cyanobacteria trends) for the specific bay — conditions vary within a single lake.
